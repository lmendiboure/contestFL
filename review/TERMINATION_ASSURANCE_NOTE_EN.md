# Termination assurance update — text for authors (not yet measured)

**Scope.** The liveness-enabled experimental Solidity contract augments the previously evaluated fixed-schema ContestFL realization with bounded submission and replacement phases. Each newly opened round stores a submission deadline; any account may invoke `expireSubmission` after that deadline. Following an accepted revision, a replacement deadline is established after the last pending challenge is closed. If neither the coordinator nor the resolver publishes the required state before that deadline, any account may invoke `expireReplacement`. Pending challenges retain their individual response deadlines and are still covered by `expireChallenge`. Both new exits fail closed (`ABORTED`) and cannot be followed by finalization.

**Meaning of termination.** Under continued chain progress, eventual inclusion of an honest watchdog's timeout or finalization transactions, finite challenge sets, and bounded retry/epoch creation, this fixes the previously identified nonterminal `SUBMIT` and `RESOLVING` paths. The guarantee is **eventual terminal outcome (`FINALIZED` or `ABORTED`)**, not successful FL recovery under arbitrary failures or asynchronous networks. The abstract DAG result remains conditional on its separate key-activation/closure/fallback assumptions; the Solidity fixed-schema contract is not a proof of a generic DAG refinement.

**Evaluation and versioning.** Existing reported v3/v4 results belong to the pre-extension contract. The separate Besu probe compiles and deploys the exact pre-extension source alongside the new source, evaluates nominal and single-correction paths using identical transactions, and measures the extra gas. Until these tests execute, no old gas figure should be silently attributed to the new contract. After execution, report the amended contract version and at least the paired gas deltas and completed liveness probes in the response to reviewers.

**Trust boundary.** This correction does not authenticate the semantics of the resolver's certificate or roots. An authorized but malicious resolver can still publish invalid claims under the experiment's trust assumption. This must be disclosed separately from liveness.
